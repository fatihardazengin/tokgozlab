I am a postdoctoral researcher in Tokgöz Lab, Sabancı University, working on next-generation wireless communication, integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), and sub-THz systems. I received my Ph.D. in 2024 from Université Gustave Eiffel in France, where my doctoral research focused on advanced signal-processing techniques for joint radar and communication systems for connected and automated vehicles.

I completed both my B.Sc. (2019) and M.Sc. (2021) studies at Université Savoie Mont Blanc in France. I received my M.Sc. in Electronics, Embedded Systems, and Telecommunications, building a multidisciplinary background spanning electronics, signal processing, embedded systems, and telecommunications.

My research interests include OFDM-based radar and ISAC, waveform design, phase noise and RF hardware impairments, mmWave and sub-THz communications, high-resolution sensing, and hardware-aware signal processing. A central theme of my work is the co-design of waveforms and transceiver architectures, with the goal of understanding how practical hardware constraints such as oscillator phase noise, power-amplifier nonlinearity, converter limitations, and RF architecture choices affect communication and sensing performance.

I am particularly interested in developing robust and energy-efficient solutions for future 6G systems that bridge theoretical signal processing, communication theory, radar sensing, and RF hardware implementation. My current research also explores waveform–hardware co-optimization, sub-THz transceiver architectures, and new approaches for improving sensing accuracy, communication reliability, and implementation efficiency in emerging wireless systems.
