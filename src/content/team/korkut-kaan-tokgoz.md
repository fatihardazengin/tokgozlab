Korkut Kaan Tokgöz is a faculty member in Electronics Engineering at the Faculty of Engineering and Natural Sciences, Sabancı University. His research brings together high-frequency integrated circuits, ultra-high-data-rate wireless communication, sensor systems, Edge AI, and IoT hardware and software.

## Education

- **Ph.D. in Physical Electronics**, Tokyo Institute of Technology, 2018. His dissertation, *Millimeter-Wave CMOS Transceiver Toward 100Gb/s Wireless Communication Systems*, was advised by Prof. Kenichi Okada and co-advised by Emeritus Prof. Akira Matsuzawa.
- **M.Eng. in Physical Electronics**, Tokyo Institute of Technology, 2014. His thesis focused on device characterization for millimeter-wave CMOS amplifiers.
- **M.Sc. in Electromagnetics**, Middle East Technical University, 2012. His thesis investigated broadband phase shifters using surface-micromachined lumped components.
- **B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronics Engineering**, Middle East Technical University, 2009, with concentrations in microwaves and antenna theory, and telecommunications.

## Academic and industry background

Tokgöz joined Sabancı University as a faculty member in January 2022. From 2019 to 2022, he was a Specially Appointed Assistant Professor at Tokyo Institute of Technology, contributing to mixed-signal, analog/RF, millimeter-wave and sub-terahertz integrated-circuit research, as well as sensor, Edge-AI, and IoT systems.

From 2018 to 2019, he worked at NEC in Kawasaki, Japan, as a Senior Researcher in the Central Research Laboratories and as an Assistant Manager in the Wireless Access Solution Division. His work included 5G power-amplifier development and high-power, high-efficiency amplifiers for fixed point-to-point wireless links. He has also contributed to research and engineering activities at the National Institute of Information and Communications Technology, TechIdea, Axelspace, and Fujitsu Laboratories in Japan.

## Research focus

His current interests include energy-efficient millimeter-wave and sub-terahertz wireless systems toward 6G; mixed-signal and analog/RF integrated circuits; de-embedding, calibration, and high-frequency device characterization; sensors and Edge-AI systems; and machine-learning-assisted modeling of active and passive semiconductor devices.
